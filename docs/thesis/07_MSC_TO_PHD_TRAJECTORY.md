# MSc-to-PhD Research Trajectory

Status: `PROPOSED LONG-TERM PROGRAMME`

If the long-term goal is progressively deeper specialization in mathematics, the geometry-centred thesis provides a natural sequence from infrastructure dynamics to advanced geometric analysis.

# Recommended MSc / early-stage kernel

## Working thesis title

**Differential and Nonsmooth Geometry of Sustainable Infrastructure Viability**

Bounded mathematical spine:

$$
\boxed{
F
\rightarrow
K_{\rm sus}
\rightarrow
\mathcal V_{\rm sus}
\rightarrow
g_\psi
\rightarrow
\rho_g
\rightarrow
T_{\mathcal V},N_{\mathcal V}
}
$$

Recommended scope:

- coupled P–W–T reduced model;
- one main hazard/stress family;
- one case study or synthetic-to-real demonstrator sequence;
- 2–4 effective continuous viability dimensions for exact/controlled computation;
- Euclidean baseline before non-Euclidean geometry;
- bounded uncertainty analysis;
- equal-resource engineering comparison.

# PhD Stage 1 — Riemannian geometry of viability

Deepen from a metric and distance to:

- geodesic structure;
- Levi-Civita connection;
- metric deformation;
- regular-boundary differential geometry;
- Jacobi-field sensitivity where justified;
- sectional/Ricci curvature only when regularity and engineering meaning are demonstrated.

Key question:

> Which intrinsic geometric structures of the viable region are stable, identifiable, and physically meaningful?

# PhD Stage 2 — Nonsmooth and variational geometry

Deepen the treatment of active-constraint intersections using:

- Clarke generalized gradients;
- proximal normals;
- contingent/tangent cones;
- coderivatives;
- set-valued differentiation;
- stratified or piecewise-smooth boundary analysis.

Key question:

> How do multiple simultaneous sustainability constraints create geometric singularities, switching surfaces, and critical directions?

# PhD Stage 3 — Geometric dynamical systems and control

For control-affine or constrained dynamics,

$$
\dot Y=f_0(Y)+\sum_i u_i f_i(Y),
$$

study:

- controllability;
- Lie brackets and distributions;
- viability-preserving control;
- state-constraint optimal control;
- dynamically feasible recovery paths.

Key warning:

$$
\text{geodesically short}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{dynamically reachable}.
$$

# PhD Stage 4 — Hamilton–Jacobi and reachability

Connect viability geometry to value functions and PDEs:

$$
\partial_tV+H(Y,\nabla V)=0.
$$

Potential directions:

- level-set representation of viable/reachable regions;
- time-varying viability under hazard forcing;
- anisotropic eikonal equations for intrinsic distance;
- links between HJ gradients and boundary geometry.

# PhD Stage 5 — Stochastic differential geometry

Move from deterministic dynamics to

$$
dY_t=f(Y_t,u_t)\,dt+\sigma(Y_t)\,dW_t.
$$

Study probabilistic viability and exit risk on manifolds:

$$
P\{Y_t\in K_{\rm sus}\ \forall t\in[0,T]\}.
$$

Potential outputs:

- probabilistic viability margin;
- uncertainty distribution over active boundaries;
- confidence in critical-interface or intervention rankings.

# PhD Stage 6 — Information geometry

Represent uncertain infrastructure state through distributions

$$
p(Y;\theta)
$$

forming a statistical manifold

$$
(\mathcal P,g_F).
$$

Candidate Fisher information metric:

$$
(g_F)_{ij}(\theta)
=
\mathbb E\left[
\partial_{\theta_i}\log p\,
\partial_{\theta_j}\log p
\right].
$$

Research question:

> Can geometry on distributions provide meaningful resilience information when state uncertainty itself becomes the central object?

This is a future extension, not a reason to replace the physical-state geometry before it is validated.

# PhD Stage 7 — Scalable computation / tensor methods

High-dimensional viability suffers from the curse of dimensionality. Candidate research directions include low-rank approximation of:

- value functions;
- viability indicators;
- reachable-set representations;
- parameterized deformation fields.

Tensor trains, sparse grids, reduced bases, or related structure-exploiting methods should be introduced only after a mathematically validated low-dimensional reference problem exists.

Do not assume a classical infrastructure state has a literal quantum tensor-product structure.

# PhD Stage 8 — Inverse sustainable infrastructure design

Move from forward analysis

$$
G\rightarrow F_G\rightarrow\mathcal V_{\rm sus}(G)
$$

toward inverse design

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal V_{\rm desired}
\rightarrow
F^\star
\rightarrow
G^\star.
}
$$

Research question:

> Given a desired sustainable viable region or required geometric margin, what infrastructure architecture and control authority can generate it under engineering and budget constraints?
