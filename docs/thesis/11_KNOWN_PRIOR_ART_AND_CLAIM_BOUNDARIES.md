# Known Prior Art and Claim Boundaries

Status: `EVIDENCE REGISTER / PARTIAL LITERATURE VALIDATION`

This document records literature that directly constrains novelty language for the proposed thesis. It is not a complete systematic review; it is a minimum prior-art boundary that must be respected in proposals, slides, and manuscripts.

# 1. Viability on Riemannian manifolds already exists

**Peng, S. & Zhu, X. (2009). _Viability property on Riemannian manifolds_. Comptes Rendus Mathématique, 347(23–24), 1423–1428.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crma.2009.10.007

Observed relevance:

- viability is formulated on a finite-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold;
- necessary/sufficient viability conditions are connected to the squared distance to the closed constraint set and a second-order PDE.

Claim boundary:

> Do **not** claim to introduce Riemannian manifolds into viability theory for the first time.

Candidate thesis contribution must instead concern a **physically calibrated Riemannian geometry of sustainable infrastructure viability**, its engineering interpretation, and its validation.

# 2. Tangential and normal conditions are foundational in viability theory

**Aubin, J.-P. (2001). _Viability Kernels and Capture Basins of Sets Under Differential Inclusions_. SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization, 40, 853–881.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1137/S036301290036968X

Observed relevance:

- viability kernels and capture basins are characterized through tangential conditions and dual normal conditions.

Claim boundary:

> Do **not** claim tangent cones or normal conditions as new mathematics in viability theory.

Candidate contribution must instead concern how smooth and nonsmooth boundary tools are specialized, calibrated, and tested for **multi-constraint coupled sustainable infrastructure**.

# 3. Stochastic viability/resilience is established

**_Extending the viability theory framework of resilience to uncertain dynamics, and application to lake eutrophication_ (2013), Ecological Indicators 29, 420–433.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2012.12.032

Observed relevance:

- viability-based resilience is extended to uncertain/stochastic dynamics.

Claim boundary:

> Do **not** claim uncertainty-aware or stochastic viability as a new concept.

Candidate contribution must be narrower: uncertainty propagation through infrastructure-specific sustainable viable geometry, geometric margin, deformation, and engineering decisions.

# 4. Hamilton–Jacobi viability under infrastructure uncertainty is now demonstrated

**_Hamilton-Jacobi reachability for viability analysis of constrained waste-to-energy systems under adversarial uncertainty_ (2026), European Journal of Control 89, 101501.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejcon.2026.101501

Observed relevance:

- a viability kernel is characterized as the zero-sublevel set of an HJ/HJBI value function;
- uncertainty/control bounds shape and shrink the viable domain;
- the application is a sustainable infrastructure/process context.

Claim boundary:

> Do **not** claim time-varying or uncertainty-deformed viability kernels in sustainable infrastructure as unprecedented.

Candidate contribution should focus on **coupled P–W–T multi-physics**, physically calibrated intrinsic geometry, smooth/nonsmooth boundary structure, cross-sector deformation, and decision value.

# 5. Digital Twins already support more than visualization

**_Digital Twin and AI Models for Infrastructure Resilience: A Systematic Knowledge Mapping_ (2025), Applied Sciences 15(24), 13135.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/app152413135

Observed relevance:

- Digital Twin and AI research includes monitoring, prediction, optimization, and infrastructure performance under stress.

**_Digital twins for hazard-resilient power grids: A systematic review and roadmap_ (2026), Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews 235, 116947.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2026.116947

Observed relevance:

- climate-aware Digital Twins are associated with predictive forecasting, energy management, resilience enhancement, restoration, and adaptive control.

Claim boundary:

> Do **not** characterize current infrastructure Digital Twins as merely 3D visualization models.

The defensible industrial gap is narrower:

$$
\text{Digital Twin state/prediction}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{explicit sustainable-viability geometry}.
$$

# 6. Prohibited overclaims

The following statements must not appear without substantially stronger evidence:

- "First use of differential geometry in resilience."
- "First viability theory on a manifold."
- "First use of tangent/normal cones for viability."
- "Existing viability theory assumes flat Euclidean space."
- "Existing resilience/control treats viability boundaries as static."
- "Digital Twins only visualize infrastructure."
- "The intrinsic viability margin is a universal distance to collapse."
- "The normal vector gives the exact control direction needed for survival."
- "A geodesic recovery path is automatically dynamically feasible."
- "More connectivity/redundancy necessarily increases sustainable viability."

# 7. Defensible candidate contribution language

Preferred formulation:

> Existing mathematical and infrastructure methods provide mature foundations in viability, reachability, differential/Riemannian geometry, nonsmooth analysis, reliability, optimization, and Digital Twins. The proposed research investigates whether a **physically calibrated smooth/nonsmooth geometry of dynamically generated sustainable viable regions in coupled P–W–T systems** can quantify deformation under hazard and coupling change and provide additional resilience diagnosis or intervention value relative to declared conventional baselines.

# 8. Review requirement

Before any novelty claim is frozen, complete a deduplicated systematic review with explicit inclusion criteria and claim-level coding across at least:

- viability/reachability in infrastructure and sustainability;
- viability on manifolds and geometric viability theory;
- nonsmooth/variational viability analysis;
- differential/Riemannian resilience metrics;
- P–W–T and interdependent-infrastructure dynamics;
- stochastic/robust viability;
- resilience optimization/control;
- Digital Twin resilience decision support.

Every final gap statement must be linked to a review record showing both supporting and contradicting/adjacent literature.
