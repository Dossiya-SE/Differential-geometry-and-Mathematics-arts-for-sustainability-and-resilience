# Candidate Gaps, Research Questions, Objectives, and Hypotheses

Status: `PROPOSED / NOT YET NOVELTY-CLOSED`

This document records the current evidence-bounded framing for the geometry-centred thesis trajectory.

# Research problem

Infrastructure resilience methods can model networks, simulate disturbances, estimate reliability, and optimize recovery. The proposed research asks a different question:

> What is the geometry of the region in which sustainable infrastructure operation remains dynamically possible, how does that geometry change under stress, and does understanding it improve engineering decisions?

The central mathematical object is

$$
\mathcal V_{\rm sus}
=
\operatorname{Viab}_{F}(K_{\rm sus})
\subset(\mathcal M,g_\psi).
$$

# Motivation

A system may be physically connected and currently acceptable while still lacking a feasible future that preserves critical services and sustainability constraints:

$$
\text{connected}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{functional}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{sustainably viable}.
$$

The research therefore moves from isolated performance scores toward a dynamically generated viable region, its intrinsic geometry, its deformation under disturbance, and its engineering meaning.

# Candidate scientific gaps

## SG1 — Physically calibrated intrinsic metric geometry

Candidate gap:

> The appropriate intrinsic metric for coupled sustainable infrastructure state spaces remains insufficiently established when distance is required to reflect physical stress, critical-service consequences, sustainability constraints, and intervention difficulty rather than coordinate-normalized Euclidean separation.

Object:

$$
Y\in(\mathcal M,g_\psi).
$$

This is **not** a claim that viability theory has never been formulated on manifolds.

## SG2 — Sustainable viability geometry

Candidate gap:

> The smooth and intrinsic geometric structure of sustainable viable regions generated by coupled nonlinear infrastructure dynamics and non-compensatory sustainability constraints remains insufficiently characterized for P–W–T systems.

Objects:

$$
\mathcal V_{\rm sus},
\qquad
\partial\mathcal V_{\rm sus},
\qquad
\rho_g.
$$

## SG3 — Smooth/nonsmooth boundary transitions

Candidate gap:

> Infrastructure sustainability constraints can become simultaneously active, producing corners, intersections, and switching surfaces that require a unified use of differential geometry and variational/nonsmooth analysis.

Objects:

$$
T_{\mathcal V}(Y),\qquad N_{\mathcal V}(Y).
$$

This is **not** a claim that tangent/normal cones are new to viability theory.

## SG4 — Geometric deformation under hazard, degradation, and coupling change

Candidate gap:

> Existing resilience analyses often report scalar degradation, reliability, connectivity, or recovery measures; the deformation of the full sustainable viable region and its intrinsic geometry under coupled multi-physics disturbances remains less mature.

Objects:

$$
\Delta\eta,\Delta\theta
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathcal V_{\rm sus}
\rightarrow
\Delta\rho_g,
\Delta T_{\mathcal V},
\Delta N_{\mathcal V}.
$$

## SG5 — Decision value of geometry

Candidate gap:

> Even if a rigorous geometric description can be computed, it remains to be established whether it adds predictive, diagnostic, or intervention value beyond Euclidean, reliability, topology, and conventional resilience baselines.

This is the principal falsification layer.

# Candidate industrial gaps

## IG1 — Threshold monitoring versus viability intelligence

Industrial systems often determine whether current limits are satisfied. They do not necessarily determine whether feasible controls exist to keep future trajectories inside a sustainable admissible region.

$$
Y\in K_{\rm sus}
\quad\text{versus}\quad
Y\in\mathcal V_{\rm sus}.
$$

## IG2 — Heterogeneous cross-sector resilience indicators

Power, water, and transportation use different engineering margins and reliability measures. A coupled system lacks a universally accepted geometric representation of system-wide sustainable operating margin.

## IG3 — State prediction without explicit viability-boundary geometry

Digital Twins and predictive models can estimate or forecast state evolution, but an explicit layer

$$
\hat Y
\rightarrow
\hat{\mathcal V}_{\rm sus}
\rightarrow
\rho_g
\rightarrow
\text{active boundary constraints}
$$

is not generally part of standard infrastructure practice.

## IG4 — Multi-constraint critical states are difficult to interpret

When several physical, service, environmental, resource, or economic limits become active together, scalar dashboards may obscure the local feasible directions and active failure mechanisms.

## IG5 — Weakly validated link from viability margin to feasible intervention

The proposed industrial translation is not simply visualization. It is a validated chain:

$$
\text{state}
\rightarrow
\text{viability geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{diagnosis}
\rightarrow
\text{dynamically feasible intervention}.
$$

# Main research objective

> Develop and validate a physically calibrated smooth/nonsmooth geometric framework for sustainable infrastructure viability; quantify how hazard, degradation, and coupling changes deform that viability geometry; and determine whether the resulting information improves resilience diagnosis and intervention relative to declared conventional baselines.

# Research questions

## RQ1 — Metric construction and calibration

> How can a physically meaningful Riemannian metric be constructed and calibrated for the reduced state space of coupled P–W–T infrastructure so that intrinsic distance reflects meaningful physical, service, and sustainability consequences?

$$
Y\in(\mathcal M,g_\psi).
$$

## RQ2 — Smooth and nonsmooth viability geometry

> What smooth and nonsmooth structures characterize the sustainable viable region generated by nonlinear P–W–T dynamics and multiple non-compensatory sustainability constraints?

$$
\mathcal V_{\rm sus},
\partial\mathcal V_{\rm sus},
T_{\mathcal V},
N_{\mathcal V}.
$$

## RQ3 — Geometric deformation under stress

> How do climate forcing, degradation, uncertainty, and cross-sector coupling changes deform the sustainable viable region and its intrinsic geometric margin?

$$
\Delta\eta,\Delta\theta
\rightarrow
\Delta\mathcal V_{\rm sus}
\rightarrow
\Delta\rho_g.
$$

## RQ4 — Decision value

> Does viability geometry provide additional predictive and intervention value beyond Euclidean, reliability, and conventional resilience measures under common disturbances and equal or explicitly normalized intervention resources?

# Hypotheses

## H1 — Physically calibrated geometry adds diagnostic information

For a predefined diagnostic performance functional \(\mathcal P\), test

$$
\mathcal P(g_\psi)
\stackrel{?}{>}
\mathcal P(g_E)+\varepsilon_{\rm meaningful}.
$$

The null possibility that the calibrated geometry adds no meaningful information is retained.

## H2 — Nonsmooth-aware analysis adds information near active-constraint intersections

Test

$$
\mathcal P(T_{\mathcal V},N_{\mathcal V})
\stackrel{?}{>}
\mathcal P(\text{smooth-only approximation}).
$$

## H3 — Physically meaningful perturbations produce deformation above numerical/UQ tolerance

For at least one declared perturbation,

$$
D_{\mathcal V}
[\mathcal V(\theta+\delta),\mathcal V(\theta)]
\stackrel{?}{>}
\varepsilon_{\rm num}+\varepsilon_{\rm UQ}.
$$

## H4 — Geometry-guided intervention improves equal-resource performance

Under an equal-resource comparison,

$$
B_{\rm geometry}=B_{\rm conventional}=B,
$$

and test

$$
\eta_{\rm geometry}
=
\frac{\Delta R_{\rm sus}^{\rm geometry}}{B}
\stackrel{?}{>}
\eta_{\rm conventional}.
$$

This is explicitly falsifiable.

# Core falsification principles

The geometry-centred thesis is weakened or rejected for a bounded application if one or more of the following hold:

1. \(g_\psi\) is not physically interpretable or is unstable to benign coordinate/scaling changes.
2. \(\rho_g\) offers no meaningful added predictive information over Euclidean or conventional margins.
3. Nonsmooth treatment produces no measurable improvement where smooth approximations are sufficient.
4. Viability deformation is dominated by numerical/UQ error rather than physical perturbation.
5. Geometry-guided interventions do not outperform or meaningfully complement conventional strategies under fair resource normalization.
