## Objective

Generate a complete RGB image from explicit mathematical fields and verify the numerical contract independently of the rendered PNG.

This experiment makes no claim that the image represents a physical system.

## Domain

The continuous domain is

$$
\Omega=[-3,3]\times[-3,3].
$$

The default discretization uses a uniform `1200 x 1200` Cartesian grid.

## Governing coordinates

$$
r(x,y)=\sqrt{x^2+y^2},
\qquad
\theta(x,y)=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x).
$$

## Annular localization

$$
A(r)=\exp[-\alpha(r-r_0)^2],
$$

with default parameters

$$
r_0=1.5,
\qquad
\alpha=12.
$$

Analytically,

$$
A(r_0)=1,
\qquad
0<A(r)\le 1.
$$

## Oscillatory phase field

$$
\phi(r,\theta)=
k_\theta\theta+k_r r+\beta\sin(m\theta),
$$

with

$$
k_\theta=10,
\quad
k_r=7,
\quad
\beta=2,
\quad
m=3.
$$

The normalized wave field is

$$
W(r,\theta)=\frac{1+\sin\phi(r,\theta)}{2},
$$

so

$$
0\le W\le1.
$$

## RGB construction

A central localization field is

$$
C(r)=e^{-3r^2}.
$$

Before clipping, the colour channels are

$$
R=0.15+0.85AW,
$$

$$
G=0.05+0.45A,
$$

$$
B=0.15+0.80A(1-W)+0.35C.
$$

The final image applies channel-wise clipping to `[0,1]`.

## Expected invariants

1. Coordinate grids have identical square shape.
2. `A(r)` is finite and positive for finite `r`.
3. `A(r_0)=1` analytically.
4. The RGB output has shape `(H, W, 3)`.
5. Every RGB value is finite.
6. Every RGB value lies in the closed interval `[0,1]`.
7. Fixed inputs and parameters produce deterministic output.
8. Invalid grid shapes or non-finite coordinates fail explicitly.

## Parameter-learning protocol

Change only one parameter per trial and predict the effect before rendering.

| Parameter | Prediction to test |
|---|---|
| `ring_radius` | moves the dominant annulus radially |
| `sharpness` | increases/decreases annulus thickness |
| `angular_frequency` | changes angular repetition around the ring |
| `radial_frequency` | changes radial phase variation |
| `modulation_amplitude` | strengthens/weakens phase warping |
| `modulation_frequency` | changes the number of angular modulation cycles |

For each trial, record whether the rendered result agrees with the prediction.

## Interpretation

The visual structure is generated by the interaction of radial localization and angular-radial oscillation. It is an equation-driven artwork and a training fixture for mathematical image synthesis.

## Known limitations

- It is not a physical simulation.
- RGB coefficients are deliberately designed rather than derived from measured radiometry.
- The default renderer samples a finite Cartesian grid and therefore approximates the underlying continuous fields.
- The PNG is an output artifact; mathematical validation is performed on the field functions, not on visual appearance alone.
